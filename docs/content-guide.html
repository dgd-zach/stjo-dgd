Before you start

A few things that apply everywhere on the site.

Log in

  1. Go to the dashboard (opens in a new tab) by adding /wp-admin to the site address (for example stjo.org/wp-admin).
  2. Enter your username and password. If you do not have a login, contact Doing Good Digital.
  3. You land on the Dashboard. The black menu down the left side is how you reach everything in this guide.

How editing works

Most content lives on Pages. Pages are built from blocks (a heading block, an image block, a paragraph block, and so on). You click into a block to change its text or swap its image. You are editing content inside a layout that is already built for you.

The golden rule

Change words and images. Do not delete or rearrange the colored bands, columns, or layout blocks. If a whole section needs to move or change shape, that is a design change: email Doing Good Digital instead of taking the layout apart.

Always preview, then update

Use Save draft and Preview to check your work in a new tab. When it looks right, click the blue Update (or Publish) button to make it live. Nothing is public until you do.

Working in the editor

A few habits that make every edit on this site faster.

Find the block you want with List View

Pages here are built from bands nested inside bands, so clicking on the page does not always land on the block you meant. Click the List View icon (three stacked lines, top left of the editor). It shows every block on the page as an outline. Click a row to select that block, then edit it in the sidebar on the right. Bands are named for what they hold ("Page Hero", "Seven Lakota Rites", and so on), so the list reads like a table of contents.

If a click selects the whole band

Most bands are locked so the layout cannot be deleted or shuffled by accident. When you click a block inside one and the whole band highlights instead, look at the block toolbar for Edit pattern and click it. You can then click the block again and edit it normally. The band itself still cannot be removed, which is the point.

Spacers own the gaps

The space between sections comes from Spacer blocks, not from the text around them. If a gap looks too big or too small, select the Spacer (List View helps) and pick Small, Medium, or Large in the sidebar. Do not delete a Spacer to close a gap; the sections will run together.

Buttons and links

  1. Click the button text to edit the label.
  2. Click the link icon in the toolbar to change where it goes. Paste a full web address, or start typing a page name to pick a page on this site.
  3. Turn on Open in new tab only for links that leave the site (the giving system, the shop, social media).

Buttons come in three looks, chosen under Styles in the sidebar: the filled button, Arrow Link (text with an arrow, used under cards and lists), and Plain Link.

Text width

Two controls change how wide text runs, and they do different things:

  • Content width on a Group block (sidebar, under Layout) sets how wide the whole section is, and it stays centered. Use this when a section, an accordion, or a row of cards looks too narrow or too wide.
  • Limit width on a paragraph, heading, or list caps that one block's line length so long text stays readable. It lines up on the left. Use it inside columns, not on whole sections.

Big numbers that count up

The large figures on Annual Financial Report are Stat Figure blocks. Type the value exactly as it should read, symbols included ($275,000, 98.6%, 1,300+). The number counts up when it scrolls into view and the symbols stay put.

Undo and revisions

Cmd+Z (Ctrl+Z on Windows) undoes your last change while the page is open. After you have saved, open Revisions in the page sidebar to compare and restore any earlier version.

Heroes & photo cards

The full-width photo at the top of a section page, and the cards with a headline over a photo. Both are Cover blocks, so they work the same way.

Change a hero photo

About, Youth Programs, Lakota Culture, Your Impact, Prayers, and Support Us open with a photo hero. Other pages open with a plain blue title band whose label and heading are ordinary text blocks.

  1. Select the hero (in List View it is the band named Page Hero).
  2. In the toolbar click Replace and choose or upload a photo. Landscape photos at least 2000px wide look best.
  3. In the sidebar, drag the Focal point dot onto the part of the photo that must stay visible (usually a face). The hero is much wider than it is tall, so the top and bottom of the photo will crop.
  4. The label and heading inside the hero are text. Click them to edit.

Photo cards

Cards with a headline over a photo appear on Home, About, Youth Programs, Your Impact, Prayers, Support Us, Our History, and Student Stories. Each card is one Cover block, and the whole card is a link.

  • Photo: select the card and click Replace in the toolbar. Use the Focal point dot to keep faces in frame.
  • Headline and link: click the text to edit it. The card's destination is the button or link inside it; change it the same way as any button.
  • Hover text: the short line under some headlines only appears when a visitor hovers or tabs to the card. In the editor it is always shown so you can edit it.
  • Dark fade: the shade behind the headline follows the Overlay opacity slider in the Styles tab. 80 is the site standard. If white text is hard to read on a light photo, raise it. The overlay colour has no effect on cards.

Info cards

The white cards with a heading, a short paragraph, and an arrow link (Your Impact, Lakota Culture, Prayers, and others) are ordinary Group blocks. Edit the text and link as usual. Keep each card's text about the same length as its neighbours so the row stays even.

Alt text applies to hero and card photos too

After replacing a photo, check the Alternative text field in the sidebar. Even when the headline says what the card is about, a short description of the picture helps ("Students in regalia at the powwow").

Links that open lightboxes

Some cards and links open a window over the page instead of going to a new page (Awards, Accreditation, Seven Lakota Rites, HAPI Homes, and others). The block is called Links that open Lightboxes.

Where the window's content comes from

Select the card and look at the sidebar. It shows one of two sources:

  • Content page: the window shows a WordPress page. Edit that page like any other and the window updates. These pages are listed in the page tree under Lightbox content pages. They are not linked in the menus.
  • Content: the text is typed straight into the block's Content field. Fine for a paragraph or two; use a content page for anything longer.

Editing a content page

  • Start headings at Heading 3. The window's own title sits above them, so the editor only offers Heading 3 to 6 on these pages.
  • If the page starts with its own heading, turn on Hide heading in lightbox on the card so the title does not appear twice.
  • Keep them short. A window that scrolls for a long time is hard to use, especially on a phone. Split long material across two cards.
  • The page's Featured image becomes the card photo and the window's top image unless the card has its own image (below).

The card's own text and image

In the sidebar, Title is the card headline and Link label is the arrow text ("Learn more", for example). The image line tells you which file is in use, either the card's own image or "Using the content page's featured image". Click Replace image to give the card its own photo, or Remove image to fall back to the featured image. The Arrow Link style hides the card and shows only the link text.

Galleries

Photo grids (for example on Attend a Powwow) use the standard Gallery block. Visitors click a photo to see it full size, then click outside it or press Escape to close.

  1. Select the gallery and use the + tile at the end to upload or pick more photos. Drag photos to reorder them.
  2. Click a photo inside the gallery. In the sidebar, under Settings, make sure Expand on click is on. New photos need this switched on to open full size.
  3. Give each photo alt text. The enlarged view announces it to screen reader users.

Expand on click also works on any single Image block, not only inside galleries.

Blog posts

News and articles. These appear on the Blog page automatically, newest first.

All posts (opens in a new tab) Add new post (opens in a new tab) Categories (opens in a new tab)
  1. Click Add new post.
  2. Type a title, then write the body. Use the + button to add images, headings, quotes, and so on.
  3. On the right, set a Category and a Featured image (this is the thumbnail people see in the listing).
  4. Add a short SEO description, then Publish.

Pages vs. posts: use a page for permanent content (About, Support Us). Use a post for dated news and stories that should stack up over time.

Images & media

Every photo and file on the site. You usually add images from inside a page or post, but you can also manage them all here.

Media library (opens in a new tab) Upload new (opens in a new tab)
Always write alt text (accessibility)

When you add an image, fill in the Alt text field with a short description of what is in the photo (for example, "Students dancing at the fall powwow"). This is what screen readers announce to visitors who cannot see the image, and it is required for accessibility. Leave it blank only for purely decorative graphics.

Keep files reasonably sized

Upload photos around 2000px wide or less. The site compresses images automatically, but starting smaller keeps pages fast.

Page SEO (Yoast)

How each page shows up in Google and when shared on social media. The site uses the Yoast SEO plugin.

Yoast dashboard (opens in a new tab)

When editing any page or post, scroll to the Yoast SEO box below the content (or open it in the sidebar). Set:

  • SEO title and Meta description: the headline and blurb Google shows.
  • Social preview: the image and text used when the page is shared on Facebook or X.
Do not change redirects or site-wide SEO settings

Per-page titles and descriptions are yours to edit. The technical settings in the Yoast dashboard (redirects, sitemaps, schema) are managed by Doing Good Digital; changing them can break search rankings.
